## Diagnosis before changes

### Exact observed failure
The current renderer constructs a bright blue box receiver at world center `(0,-0.72,0)` with scale `(4,.02,2)` (`src/renderer-facade.js`, `createShadowLight`). The source scene defines the playfield rectangle X `[-2,2]`, Z `[-1.5,1.5]`, and floorY `-.72` (`src/gameplay-scene-model.js`). Therefore the receiver is one world unit short in Z and its top face is `-.71`, above the nominal floorY. Independent GLB inspection subsequently established that the actual blue-glass playfield top is `-.80` and bottom `-.86`; the opaque receiver at `-.71` hides most of its glass and cyan edges. The correction must be placed beneath `-.86`, not merely at nominal `floorY=-.72`. The normal test gate also fails: `node scripts/validate-equipment-shadows.js` asserts `shadow light uses directional type` because it expects obsolete `pc.LIGHTTYPE_DIRECTIONAL` and `pc.SHADOW_PCSS_32F` source tokens; production uses `type:"directional"` and `pc.SHADOW_PCF3_32F`.

### Expected behavior and execution path
The attached renderer creates one directional light and shadow-receiving geometry in `createShadowLight`, then each frame stages flow saber/boxing glove GLBs (casting shadows) against the playfield from the actual camera. The receiver should cover the complete 4×3 XZ playfield, with its visible upper surface below the actual glass-track bottom at `floorY-.14`, and screenshots from top, 3/4, and side should show matching boundaries and no raised receiver.

### Root cause and alternatives
Geometry coordinates are independently hard-coded, not derived from the scene's canonical bounds. Recent commits repeatedly guessed at position, size, and orientation (plane rotation, then 4×2 box), rather than checking the XZ rectangle and top-face equation. The measured source-level mismatch is definite. An independent audit of the pinned GLB found actual track top `floorY-.08` and bottom `floorY-.14`; opaque World-layer receiver above it causes track occlusion, confirmed qualitatively by first real but CSS-contaminated captures showing a dark rectangular slab over cyan track. Whether shadows remain sufficiently readable through glass with the receiver below it requires valid new captures. Alternative hypotheses: authored track geometry occludes the receiver; directional light or caster assets do not contribute visible shadows; blue emissive debug material masks the shadow. Multi-angle real WebGL captures and a behavioral oracle distinguish these.
