## Diagnostic report (before source changes)
**Exact observed failure:** Derrick's 497×411 Glitch screenshot shows translucent red obstacle panels and strong rectangular dark bars directly beneath them, despite apparently also having cast-like shadowing. Derrick reports regular beats have **no shadow at all** in the just-served preview. In the current source `src/gameplay-scene-model.js` obstacle branches still append a `kind:"shadow"` box; its non-obstacle branch emits only icon(s) after the prior edit. `src/renderer-facade.js:595` instantiates every pooled gameplay GLB with `{castShadows:false,receiveShadows:false}`. These source facts are direct. Whether a second obstacle shadow in the screenshot is true native casting versus transparent wall blending is not established without a framebuffer toggle; do not assert it as fact.

**Expected behavior:** Notes/punches/guards/bombs and continuous glass obstacles darken the shadow-only receiver from their authored GLB geometry, with no fake rectangle below either class. Enabling and disabling their mesh-instance `castShadow` while keeping the object drawn should change only receiver-shadow pixels. No-caster receiver parity remains exact.

**Execution path:** `buildGameplaySceneModel` calls the obstacle `targetObjects` branches to emit wall plus fake shadow; for beats it emits GLB icon only. `renderGameplayScene` sends those objects to `updateSceneObjects`; wall/note assets use `acquireAssetEntity`, which requests `castShadows:false`, so no GLB contributes to the directional light's shadow map. The fake obstacle mesh reaches a pooled primitive and paints a flat rectangle; no beat fake exists. The receiver's multiplicative material can only multiply sampled *caster* visibility, so currently these beats have no darkening even though the receiver is present.

**Most likely root cause:** Prior scope removed beat impostors but deliberately retained obstacle impostors without enabling native casting for target asset entities. The target pool's cast-disabled setting is the missing link between visual target and shadow map. Installed PlayCanvas inspection also found the directional light's `layers` excludes the Aero Gameplay Targets layer where beats render; enabling their caster flag alone will still fail. Chromium baseline A/B now resolves the screenshot ambiguity: wall GLB mesh casters are false and caster-toggle delta is zero; the remaining fake wall primitive itself contributes a dark shadow-map silhouette (receiver-toggle darkens 445/1511/1750 pixels across top/three-quarter/side). The visible double effect is a fake rectangle plus its own unintended native cast, not two independent shadows from the GLB.

**Alternative hypotheses (ranked):** (1) Transparent GLB wall materials might be excluded or alpha-rejected by PlayCanvas shadow pass even if `castShadows:true`; inspect installed engine and test rendered wall A/B. (2) Beat icon might sit outside the directional light's shadowDistance/frustum; test near-athlete records at Y>receiver and multiple poses. (3) The pinned GLB geometry and authored material opacity could produce very faint silhouette at 40% intensity; measure receiver-only pixel deltas and human screenshots. (4) Browser cache loaded old asset despite force refresh; inspect live preview stamp/bundle and reproduce exact current source fixture before blaming caching.

**Why previous fix failed:** It removed non-obstacle fake scene objects and correctly preserved wall fake objects under the earlier narrow interpretation, but did not connect target GLBs to the shadow map. It also did not assert actual *target* cast pixels; equipment-only shadow oracle stayed green. Earlier screenshot review showed equipment, not beat/wall casters. A new end-to-end target visual oracle is required.

**Resolved unknowns:** the perceived extra wall shadow came from the fake wall rectangle also casting through the directional light, not from the wall GLB. Translucent `mat/red_glass` has authored forward opacity .24 but default shadow pass ignores that opacity; using Bayer8 shadow dither on its cloned material retains the visible wall and yields a partial shadow, proven with same-frame WebGL A/B. Near-athlete Flow and Boxing casts are visible in all three tested cameras. **Remaining uncertainty:** frame-time cost on physical Glitch/mobile devices with many simultaneous targets, tracked in benchmark Bead `aerobeat-web-assembly-fapz`. **Minimal reproduction:** isolated loaded Flow note and wall in Chromium: baseline has no note shadow and a fake wall primitive. With the fix, caster-off or receiver-off removes the new native silhouettes without removing the visible GLB body; no-target scene is pixel-identical with/without receiver.

**Proposed verification:** First demonstrate failing baseline (note target has zero shadow-mask delta when cast flag toggled/receiver receive toggled; wall fake box remains), then remove wall fake and enable selective GLB casters. Validate same-frame shadow-on/off pixel deltas beneath note, punch, guard, bomb, wall in top/three-quarter/side; ensure off-target pixels and no-caster frame unchanged; assert all scene model `kind:"shadow"` counts zero and all target mesh casters true, track casters false. Capture and inspect user-relevant top/three-quarter/side and a composition resembling Glitch screenshot. Normal `npm test` and `npm run test:browser` should execute visual assertions before separately tracked `m0cb` weave failure. Production assembly browser proof and tailnet HTTPS boot are required before serving.

**Recommended smallest fix:** Remove the two obstacle `sceneObject(...,"shadow")` branches; selectively instantiate live icon and wall asset pool entries as native shadow casters (not track/environment, feedback, aftermath, collider overlays). If translucent wall shader cannot cast safely, use a caster-only proxy aligned to authored GLB without visible fake shadow *on the floor* and prove geometry/opacity under actual WebGL. Ensure pooled mixed-use asset IDs cannot leak cast flags across object kinds. Avoid native shadow conversion for entire scene indiscriminately.
